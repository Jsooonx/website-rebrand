# PHASE — Design Brief

## Project summary

PHASE is a fictional independent creative-production studio based in
Jakarta and working remotely. It helps ambitious culture, hospitality, fashion,
music, and consumer-technology brands turn an uncertain next step into a clear
identity, campaign, motion system, or digital experience.

The site must feel sharp, culturally aware, and high-craft without becoming a
generic futuristic-agency template. Its job is to establish the studio's point
of view quickly, show selected work, and convert qualified visitors into
project enquiries.

### Useful principles to retain

- A full-viewport opening composition with a decisive image and oversized
  wordmark creates immediate presence.
- Small, precise interface typography provides useful counterweight to a
  large editorial gesture.
- An asymmetrical grid can still feel ordered when small elements align to
  consistent edge margins and columns.
- A short service index and restrained UI details make the work feel
  operational rather than decorative.
- A broad, quiet About-page statement followed by an image, compact evidence,
  and moving logo cloud creates a persuasive manifesto-to-proof rhythm.
- A persistent portfolio rail and sequential, vertically advancing project
  cards help visitors focus on one visual story at a time.
- A near-static services catalogue can feel premium through precise typography,
  generous whitespace, and a strictly repeated information grid.
- Three clearly differentiated cards can make an engagement decision legible,
  provided their scope, timeframe, and price model are explicit.
- Giving one testimonial a full editorial moment can make qualitative proof
  more credible than a dense, generic quote carousel.
- A continuous marquee behind a fixed image can give a final conversion CTA
  momentum when the actual action remains clear and static.
- A restrained footer reveal can give the last scroll movement depth without
  turning the page ending into an inaccessible animation.

### Deliberate departures

- Use an original name, copy, wordmark, palette, image treatment, and layout.
- Replace the warm red/orange palette with ink black, bone, acid lime, and
  electric blue.
- The hero image should occupy a deliberate right-hand frame rather than being
  a full-screen background with overlaid copy.
- Make the primary CTA explicit and accessible; avoid low-contrast text as a
  substitute for mood.
- Avoid copying the reference's logo geometry, portrait blur, plus marks,
  card treatment, or exact navigation structure.

## Brand and positioning

**Name:** PHASE

**Positioning:** Independent creative production for brands making their next
move.

**Primary audience:** Founders, brand leaders, and creative directors at
ambitious, design-literate teams.

**Primary conversion:** Start a project enquiry.

**Supporting conversion:** View selected work and understand the studio's
capabilities.

**Brand voice:** Concise, confident, observant, and specific. Never use empty
superlatives such as "world-class" or "disruptive". Prefer direct language
that connects a creative decision to a business or cultural outcome.

## Homepage structure

1. **Hero** — value proposition, primary CTA, service index, featured campaign
   image, and oversized PHASE display mark.
2. **Selected phases** — exactly four varied case studies, with intentionally
   mixed media aspect ratios and concise outcome-led captions.
3. **Capabilities** — Identity, Campaigns, Motion, and Digital; each has a
   short description and a relevant proof point.
4. **Studio approach** — a compact explanation of how a phase moves from
   question to release.
5. **People / network** — a human view of the independent studio and its
   collaborators, without a generic team-grid cliché.
6. **Closing CTA** — a project-fit prompt, a moving marquee treatment, and an
   accessible enquiry route.
7. **Footer** — footer-reveal ending with location, email, navigation, legal
   details, and a large PHASE wordmark.

## About page structure

1. **Statement** — an oversized, specific statement of PHASE's purpose;
   it must describe the studio's point of view, not a design style.
2. **Working evidence** — an original editorial image or artefact from the
   studio process, paired with concise text under Signal, System, and Release.
3. **Proof** — selected, contextual facts such as repeat collaborators,
   industries, launch cadence, or project range. Do not use unqualified claims
   such as a 100% success rate.
4. **Partner logo cloud** — an infinitely looping strip of custom SVG partner
   marks, moving from right to left.
5. **Contact CTA** — `Start a project ↗`, describing the kind of brief that is
   a good fit.

### About-page statement

`We make the next move visible—before the market asks for it.`

### Working principles

- **Signal** — find the creative decision that matters.
- **System** — turn the decision into an identity and campaign that holds up.
- **Release** — bring the work into the world through motion, digital, and
  launch support.

## Selected phases

The homepage portfolio shows exactly four original fictional case studies. It
uses a sticky-scrolling composition on desktop: a compact left rail remains in
view while the project cards advance upward in a single right-hand column.

### Sticky rail

- Label: `Selected phases · 01—04`.
- Statement: `Four moves, made visible.`
- Active project indicator: `01 / 04`, updating as each card becomes the
  primary card in the viewport.
- Link: `View all work ↗`.
- The rail is informative, not a scroll-jacking control. Natural browser
  scrolling always remains intact.

### Project cards

Each card is a normal, keyboard-accessible link to a case-study detail page.
It includes a project index, original campaign image, project name, discipline,
and year. The four cards must have intentionally varied aspect ratios and
image compositions, rather than a uniform gallery grid.

Suggested initial case-study set:

| Index | Project | Discipline | Image direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | Aeru House | Brand identity + launch campaign | Sunlit architecture; pale mineral tones |
| 02 | Sora Objects | E-commerce + art direction | Close product crops; cobalt and chrome |
| 03 | Umami Radio | Identity + motion system | Night performance stills; saturated lime accent |
| 04 | Paloma Hotel | Digital editorial + campaign | Warm hospitality scenes; tactile grain |

### Interaction and responsive rules

- On desktop, cards move naturally through the viewport while the rail uses
  `position: sticky`; do not pin the full page or hijack wheel/trackpad input.
- Update the active index using viewport visibility, with no abrupt transforms
  or forced snap between projects.
- A card's hover state may reveal a short project descriptor or gently shift
  its image crop. Its title and purpose must remain understandable without
  hover.
- On tablet, release the sticky rail before it crowds the cards.
- On mobile and with `prefers-reduced-motion`, render a conventional vertical
  list: heading first, then the four cards in document order. Do not retain a
  tall pinned scene or scroll-linked animation.

## Services page

The Services page is intentionally quiet. It earns its premium feeling through
type scale, spacing, image selection, and alignment—not through a sticky scene
or elaborate scroll-linked effects.

### Intro

**Page label:** `Capabilities · 03`

**Headline:**

`From the first signal to the final release, we build the systems that make a
brand move.`

The heading is a large, editorial sans-serif composition on an Ink background.
Use Bone for the primary phrase and Slate only for a clearly secondary clause.
An original small-format image may interrupt the first line, but must not echo
the reference's image placement, crop, or colour treatment.

### Service catalogue

The catalogue has exactly four entries. Each desktop row uses a three-part
composition: oversized index at left, original image in the middle, and the
service title, outcome statement, and concise scope at right. Thin rules divide
the rows. The layout may change image aspect ratio between entries while keeping
the alignment system stable.

| Index | Service | Outcome statement | Concise scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | Identity | Give the brand a usable point of view. | Positioning, verbal direction, identity system |
| 02 | Campaigns | Turn the point of view into a moment people notice. | Creative direction, concepts, launch assets |
| 03 | Motion | Make the system move with purpose. | Motion language, film, social-first movement |
| 04 | Digital | Build a destination that turns attention into action. | Editorial websites, product moments, digital experiences |

### Layout, interaction, and accessibility

- Each service is a semantic article with a clear heading; the entire row may
  link to a detail page only when such a page exists.
- Keep descriptions legible at WCAG AA contrast. Slate is for secondary text,
  never for essential information against Ink.
- Use only a modest in-view opacity/translation reveal and a restrained image
  crop shift on hover. The page must remain visually complete with all motion
  removed.
- Avoid decorative markers copied from the reference. Use PHASE's thin
  rules, compact metadata, and acid-lime sparingly for focus or active states.
- On mobile, stack each row as index, image, then text; do not reduce the
  oversized index so far that it loses its cataloguing role.

## Engagements page

The pricing surface is named **Engagements**, reflecting how a creative
production studio works. It presents three ways to work with PHASE rather
than pretending every identity or campaign is a comparable subscription plan.

### Engagement cards

Use three responsive cards with a restrained Bone / Ink treatment. The Studio
Partner card is dark and visually featured, but must not imply that it is the
right choice for every visitor. A concise `How pricing works` note follows the
cards to explain that final fees are confirmed after scope, timing, and team
requirements are agreed.

| Engagement | Best for | Timeframe | Price model | Core scope | CTA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Signal Sprint | Teams that need clarity before a major decision | 2 weeks | Fixed scope · starting from | Stakeholder session, positioning direction, decision memo | `See sprint scope ↗` |
| Launch Phase | A brand or campaign that needs to be built and released | 6–8 weeks | Fixed project · starting from | Creative direction, identity or campaign system, launch assets | `Plan a launch ↗` |
| Studio Partner | Teams that need continuous senior creative direction | Monthly | Retainer · scoped together | Prioritised creative support, planning, design direction, delivery oversight | `Discuss a partnership ↗` |

### Pricing rules

- Do not display invented exact prices, currencies, or claims. Show `Starting
  from` only after PHASE's real market, currency, and commercial terms are
  approved.
- Each card states a timeframe, key deliverables, and how scope changes are
  handled. It never uses a generic feature checklist that applies identically
  to every tier.
- Explain revision rounds or review rhythm in the detailed scope, not in
  fine-print hidden below the CTA.
- Keep every CTA descriptive and route it to the relevant scope detail or
  enquiry form.

### Layout and accessibility

- Price or pricing model is the dominant information within each card, followed
  by timeframe and scope. Avoid visually burying commercial qualifications.
- Cards are not a tab widget and do not require hover to reveal key details.
- On narrow screens, stack the cards in document order; preserve the featured
  card's contrast without changing its reading order.
- Use modest hover and focus states only. The layout should carry the entire
  experience without animation.

## Testimonials page

The Testimonials page presents exactly three curated quotes, one at a time. It
is an editorial proof surface, not a continuously moving cloud of small cards.
Every quote must relate to a selected PHASE project and describe a real,
specific result or working experience.

### Testimonial composition

- **Left column:** original partner mark or project image crop, plus previous
  and next chevron controls.
- **Main column:** a large testimonial quote, set for comfortable reading;
  keep quotes concise enough to avoid becoming a wall of type.
- **Proof row:** name, role, organisation, project, engagement type, and year.
  Use project context rather than unexplained satisfaction percentages or
  inflated success counts.
- **Progress indicator:** `01 / 03`, visible beside or between the controls.

### Planned testimonial set

| Index | Partner | Project context | Evidence focus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | Maya Santosa · Founder, Aeru House | Identity + Launch · 2026 | Strategic clarity and a decisive launch system |
| 02 | Theo Lim · Creative Director, Sora Objects | E-commerce + Art Direction · 2026 | A coherent product world across campaign and commerce |
| 03 | Rani Kusuma · Brand Lead, Paloma Hotel | Digital Editorial + Campaign · 2026 | A digital experience that reflected the physical hospitality offer |

All names, organisations, quotations, and results remain fictional until
replaced by approved real client material. Do not imply endorsements from real
people or companies without permission.

### Interaction and accessibility

- The carousel never auto-advances. Previous and next chevrons are explicit
  buttons with accessible labels: `Previous testimonial` and `Next
  testimonial`.
- Buttons support keyboard focus and standard `Enter` / `Space` activation.
  Left and right arrow keys may provide an optional shortcut when the carousel
  itself has focus, but are never required for use.
- On change, use a restrained 250–400 ms fade or directional transition. Do
  not shift keyboard focus into the incoming quote automatically.
- Announce the updated index and quote context through an appropriately scoped
  live region, without reading a long quote repeatedly during incidental
  interaction.
- With `prefers-reduced-motion`, switch quotes without animated movement.
- On mobile, retain one quote at a time and place the controls directly below
  the quote, with touch targets of at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels.

## Closing CTA

The final homepage section appears immediately before the footer. It gives the
visitor a memorable final visual moment while maintaining one clear action.

### Content

**Semantic heading:** `Make the next move visible.`

**Marquee phrase:** `MAKE THE NEXT MOVE VISIBLE → START A PROJECT →`

**CTA:** `Start a project ↗`

**Supporting line:** `Tell us what needs to move. We’ll reply with the right
next step.`

### Composition

- Use an original full-bleed editorial background image with a reliable Ink
  overlay, so text and controls stay legible across the full image.
- Place the oversized marquee behind a centred original PHASE image card.
  The card may show a collaborator, proof sheet, or tactile studio artefact;
  it must not reproduce the supplied reference's portrait or crop.
- The static card contains the supporting line and CTA. It is the interaction
  anchor; the marquee remains a visual layer behind it.
- Avoid decorative symbols copied from the reference. The visual system's thin
  rules and restrained acid-lime accent are sufficient.

### Motion and accessibility

- Build the marquee as a duplicated, non-interactive text track translated
  horizontally from right to left with `transform`, creating a seamless loop.
- Maintain a calm 25–35 second cycle on desktop. The marquee is atmosphere,
  not a reading task.
- Render the semantic heading and CTA as normal document content; no critical
  information exists only within the moving track.
- At `prefers-reduced-motion`, hide or stop the duplicated track and show a
  single static headline. The background image, supporting line, and CTA
  remain unchanged.
- On mobile, retain the static heading and CTA but simplify the image-card
  composition; do not let the marquee create horizontal overflow or obscure
  controls.

## Footer

The footer is the final brand composition. It uses an Ink background, generous
space, a large Bone PHASE wordmark, and concise contact/navigation details.
It follows the Closing CTA as a footer-reveal layer rather than an ordinary
static end cap on desktop.

### Content

**Contact line:** `Let’s make the next move.`

**Email:** `hello@phase.studio` *(placeholder; replace before public
launch)*

**Navigation:** Home `01`, Work `02`, About `03`, Services `04`, Engagements
`05`.

**Metadata:** `Jakarta / Everywhere · © 2026`.

Use only confirmed social links and legal information at implementation time.

### Footer reveal and parallax

- On desktop, the footer is the lower visual layer. The preceding main content
  has an opaque surface and a higher stacking order; as it scrolls upward out
  of view, it reveals the footer beneath.
- Prefer a layout-safe footer reveal such as a sticky bottom layer with a main
  content surface that reserves the footer's full height. Do not use a fixed
  footer that can cover the last content, focus target, or browser zoomed view.
- Limit depth movement to one non-essential layer, such as the oversized
  wordmark or a thin separator, at roughly 5–10% of scroll distance. Text,
  email, and navigation stay stable.
- Preserve natural browser scrolling; never capture wheel, touch, or trackpad
  input to simulate the effect.
- On mobile and at `prefers-reduced-motion`, render the footer directly after
  the Closing CTA with no sticky reveal or parallax transform.

### Accessibility

- The email is a standard `mailto:` link and navigation uses regular anchor
  links in a semantic `nav` with an accessible label.
- Keep focus-visible states high contrast against Ink and ensure the giant
  wordmark does not overlap links at any viewport size or zoom level.
- Maintain logical document and tab order whether or not the reveal treatment
  is active.

## Visual system

### Colour

| Token | Value | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ink | `#0B0D10` | Main background and dark text |
| Bone | `#F5F2EA` | Light surfaces and primary text on ink |
| Acid lime | `#D7FF3F` | CTA, states, and selected emphasis |
| Electric blue | `#5BA7FF` | Sparse media and information accent |
| Slate | `#9299A3` | Secondary metadata only |

Acid lime is functional emphasis, not a blanket decoration. It should occupy a
small portion of any viewport.

### Typography

- Use a neutral sans-serif UI family such as Geist or Inter for navigation,
  body, labels, and controls.
- Use a heavy, wide sans-serif display face such as Archivo Expanded for large
  headlines and the custom wordmark treatment.
- Treat the PHASE display mark as a bespoke typographic composition; it
  must not imitate the reference's letterforms.
- Use tabular figures for indexes, timestamps, and availability metadata.

### Imagery

- Photography is editorial and tactile: hard flash, considered crops, subtle
  film grain, and shadows with visible depth.
- Use real, original, or properly licensed assets only. Store production-ready
  derivatives in the runtime asset path and source files outside it.
- Do not use the supplied reference image in the live website.
- Avoid uniform card crops; each case study's aspect ratio should support the
  material it represents.

### Graphic language

- Thin rules, crop frames, and time/phase metadata support the production
  theme.
- Interface elements are flat and crisp, with a limited use of radius.
- Do not add decorative sci-fi glyphs, random grids, or motion effects unless
  they communicate status, sequence, or interaction.

### Partner logo cloud

- Create a set of original, fictional partner marks as clean inline SVGs. Each
  mark should be simple, single-colour, optically balanced, and distinct in
  silhouette; they must not imitate real company logos or the supplied
  reference's marks.
- Set the marks in a muted Bone-on-Ink or Ink-on-Bone treatment according to
  the section background. Cards should be avoided unless they serve a layout
  purpose; the cloud itself is the primary visual.
- Render enough duplicated sequence width to make the animation seamless, then
  translate the complete track continuously from right to left at a calm,
  readable speed (roughly 35–50 seconds per cycle on desktop).
- Pause the loop when a keyboard user focuses the cloud; provide a visible
  focus treatment only if individual marks are links. Do not make logos links
  without meaningful destinations.
- With `prefers-reduced-motion`, remove the animation and show a static,
  horizontally scrollable or wrapping collection with the same information.

## Layout and responsive behaviour

### Desktop

- Hero fills at least the first viewport; use a 12-column grid with generous
  outer margins and a compact header.
- Place copy on the left, a framed campaign image on the right, and the large
  PHASE mark across the lower portion of the composition.
- Keep the primary CTA within the initial viewport.
- Navigation is visible and includes a clearly styled project CTA.

### Tablet

- Reduce the hero grid to eight columns while retaining the copy/image
  relationship.
- Allow the display mark to wrap only at an intentional break.
- Preserve readable type and usable target sizes rather than scaling the
  desktop layout proportionally.

### Mobile

- Use a compact header with wordmark and menu control.
- Place headline and CTA before the image. The image uses a 4:5 or similarly
  purposeful crop.
- Render the service index as a concise two-column list or horizontal scroller.
- Keep the display mark large but prevent it from obscuring controls or
  requiring horizontal scrolling.

## Motion and interaction

- Introduce hero layers in reading order: eyebrow and headline, image, then
  display mark and supporting metadata.
- Use quick editorial reveals (roughly 300–600 ms) and modest stagger; never
  use a slow typewriter treatment for normal copy.
- Image hover may shift the crop or reveal a project label subtly. Do not use
  aggressive 3D tilt.
- Buttons and links need clear hover, focus-visible, active, and touch states.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: show content immediately and remove
  non-essential transforms, staggers, and continuous effects.

### Micro-interaction system

Interactive feedback is precise and material rather than playful. Reuse a small
number of behaviours across the site so that PHASE feels like one system.

| Surface | Hover / focus behaviour | Press / state behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text CTA and inline link | Label shifts 2–4px right; arrow moves slightly farther; underline reveals from left to right | Clear `:focus-visible` outline; no layout shift |
| Primary CTA | Ink/Bone or Acid-lime surface transition; label and arrow move as a unit | `scale(0.96)` while pressed, then return via an interruptible transition |
| Navigation | Index brightens before a thin underline appears under the label | Current page remains visibly active without hover |
| Work card | Image crop scales to at most `1.02`; metadata gains opacity; title underlines | The complete card remains a normal keyboard-accessible link |
| Service row | Index takes Acid-lime; image crop shifts 1–2% | No full-row lift or shadow that breaks the catalogue grid |
| Testimonial chevron | Arrow translates 2–3px toward its direction | `scale(0.96)` on press; disabled state is legible |
| Footer email | Bone-to-Acid-lime colour shift with an underline sweep | Standard `mailto:` focus state |

- Small state changes use approximately 140 ms; CTA/card hovers use 180–220
  ms; testimonial changes use 250–400 ms. Always transition explicit
  properties, never `transition: all`.
- Add high-contrast `:focus-visible` styles that match every hover cue. Compact
  controls have at least a 40 by 40 CSS-pixel target on desktop and 44 by 44 on
  touch screens.
- Use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` for changing indexes and counters.
  Use a low-opacity pure-white image outline on Ink and pure-black outline on
  Bone surfaces.
- Interaction motion must be interruptible CSS transitions. Reserve keyframes
  for one-shot entrance sequences and continuously moving decorative tracks.

### Entrance choreography

Entrance motion follows content type, so no two sections perform the same
generic reveal. Trigger each sequence once when the section is meaningfully in
view; do not replay it every time a visitor scrolls past.

| Content type | Entrance treatment | Timing and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | Header details settle first, then headline words resolve, then image and metadata arrive | Groups stagger by about 100 ms; headline words by 45–60 ms; complete hero reveal in under 900 ms |
| Long editorial paragraph | **Word-level blur resolve:** each word moves up 8–12px while opacity rises and blur resolves from 4px to 0px | 35–50 ms word stagger; 260–320 ms per word; reserve for one key paragraph per page |
| Short section title | A compact masked line reveal, followed by eyebrow/metadata fading in from 8px below | 300–420 ms; do not split a short title into every word if the paragraph already uses a word reveal |
| Image or project card | Image settles from a 1.04 crop scale with opacity, then its caption rule draws across | 400–520 ms; transform and opacity only; no large fly-in |
| Services catalogue | Each row enters as its oversized index, image, and copy resolve in a three-beat sequence | 80 ms between the three elements; rows activate only near the viewport, never all at once |
| Sticky work section | Cards are already animated by natural scroll progression; only the active index crossfades | No secondary entrance animation on every card, preventing visual competition with scrolling |
| Testimonials | Quote block fades/settles first; identity and project context follow | 250–400 ms; chevron direction determines a subtle 12px exit/enter direction |
| Closing CTA | Background appears first, static card resolves next, then the decorative marquee begins | Marquee is delayed until content is readable; it never blocks the CTA |
| Footer reveal | Main content reveals the footer as described below; footer information itself stays still | A minimal wordmark/separator depth shift only |

#### Word-level blur reveal requirements

- Apply this treatment only to selected long-form editorial copy, never to
  navigation, button labels, prices, legal text, or every paragraph on a page.
- Split only the visual presentation into word spans. Preserve one complete
  semantic sentence for assistive technology so it is announced naturally,
  rather than as a succession of isolated words.
- Preserve spaces and line wrapping during splitting; use `text-wrap: pretty`
  for body copy and test at narrow widths to prevent awkward single-word lines.
- At `prefers-reduced-motion`, show the final sharp text immediately. Also show
  the final state immediately when JavaScript is unavailable or an entrance
  observer fails.
- Do not apply a blur reveal to text that a user needs to act on immediately.
  The goal is a quick editorial settling effect, not delayed reading.

## Accessibility and performance requirements

- Use semantic landmarks, one logical `h1`, descriptive links, and keyboard
  operable navigation.
- Ensure body and interactive-text contrast meets WCAG AA; mood must not
  reduce legibility.
- Do not convey meaning solely through colour or motion.
- Give images meaningful alt text, or empty alt text only when they are purely
  decorative.
- Reserve image space to avoid layout shift, serve responsive images, and
  avoid loading non-critical media eagerly.

## Implementation notes

The approved stack is Vite, React with TypeScript/TSX, Tailwind CSS, Framer
Motion, and Lenis smooth scrolling. The experience is implemented as a
single-page editorial studio site with semantic section anchors, progressive
enhancement, and static fallbacks for reduced motion.
